# How to Negotiate a Raise and Actually Get It
## A Guide for Nigerian Bank Employees

---

## Slide 1: Why This Matters

### A ₦100,000 raise doesn't just change this year.
### It changes every year after it.

**Your base. Your bonuses. Your RSA Pension contributions.**

One conversation — compounding for decades.

| Impact Area | How It Compounds |
|-------------|------------------|
| **Higher Base** | Every future raise builds on it |
| **Bigger Bonuses** | Most are 10-25% of base salary |
| **More at Retirement** | RSA Pension contributions scale with income |

## Slide 2: Know Your Number Before You Walk In

### 01 Research the market
Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, PayScale, and industry peers. What does your role actually pay in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt — not what you think it pays.

**Junior Bank Roles (2026 Market Range):**
- Relationship Officer: ₦150k - ₦300k/month
- Operations Officer: ₦120k - ₦250k/month
- Customer Service Officer: ₦100k - ₦200k/month

### 02 Factor in your context
Your city, your experience level, your results. The number isn't generic — it's specific to you.

### 03 Pick a number, then go higher
Whatever you land on, ask for slightly above it. You'll almost never be told you asked too low.

💡 **Pro tip:** If you haven't looked at market data in the last 6 months, you don't know your number yet. Salaries shift. Markets move. What felt fair two years ago may no longer be.

## Slide 3: Build the Case Around Value, Not Need

### Nobody gets a raise because rent went up.
### They get it because they proved they're worth more.

**Banking-Specific Value Metrics:**

💰 **Revenue Generated**
- New accounts opened: "Brought in 45 new customers, ₦12M in deposits"
- Loan products sold: "Processed ₦25M in personal loans, 15% above target"
- Cross-selling success: "Increased product holding ratio from 1.8 to 2.3 per customer"

💵 **Costs Saved**
- Reduced transaction errors by 30%
- Streamlined account opening process (cut time by 40%)
- Reduced customer complaints through proactive service

🔧 **Problems Solved**
- Recovered dormant accounts worth ₦5M
- Led branch migration to new banking software
- Trained 8 new team members, reducing onboarding time

📋 **Scope Expanded**
- Took on acting supervisor duties for 3 months
- Managing both retail and SME portfolios now
- Handling reconciliations beyond your job description

## Slide 4: Timing the Conversation

### When you ask matters almost as much as what you ask for.

| Timing | Why It Works | Banking Context |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| ✅ **Right after a win** | A major deal closed. Branch hit quarterly target. You resolved a complex customer issue. Strike while the impact is fresh. | Post-quarter when targets are met |
| ✅ **Before review cycles** | Get ahead of the process. By the time your review arrives, the budget is often decided. | Talk 6-8 weeks before annual reviews |
| ✅ **Post-audit period** | After successful CBN/internal audits when the bank is celebrating compliance wins | Q2/Q3 when budgets aren't frozen |
| ✅ **When bank is doing well** | Strong earnings reports, expansion announcements, branch growth | After good financial year results |
| ❌ **During rough patches** | Cost-cutting mode, branch restructuring, economic downturn | Read the room carefully |

## Slide 5: Inside the Conversation

### Step 1: Open with your value
Start with what you've contributed — not what you need.

**Example:** "Over the past year, I've grown my portfolio by ₦18M and consistently exceeded my targets by 20%. I'd like to discuss my compensation to reflect this performance."

### Step 2: Anchor with your number
Say it clearly. Don't hedge, don't give a range first.

**Example:** "Based on my performance and market research for relationship officers in Lagos, I'm requesting an increase to ₦250,000 monthly."

### Step 3: Don't explain or apologize
Over-justifying signals insecurity. State your case, make the ask, and stop talking.

**Avoid:** "I know times are hard and I don't want to be pushy but..."
**Instead:** "This reflects the value I'm bringing to the branch."

### Step 4: Let the silence sit
After the ask, pause. The discomfort is on their side too. Don't rescue them from it.

## Slide 6: Navigating Banking Hierarchy & Culture

### Respect the chain of command
- Start with your direct supervisor, not their boss
- Request a formal meeting (don't ambush them)
- Come with documentation ready

### Have your evidence organized
- **Performance reviews** (last 2-3 cycles)
- **Target achievement records** (print from system)
- **Customer feedback/commendations**
- **Additional certifications** (CIBN, Excel training, etc.)

### Professional approach matters
- Dress formally for the meeting
- Choose timing when your manager isn't stressed
- Frame it as a partnership: "I want to continue growing with the bank"

### Know your bank's policies
- Check HR manual for raise eligibility criteria
- Understand your grade level and next step
- Know the typical raise percentage (usually 10-20%)

## Slide 7: When They Say No

### No isn't the end of the conversation — it's the beginning of the next one.

**If there's a path forward:**
- ✅ Ask: "What would need to change for this to be a yes?"
- ✅ Get a specific timeline: "If I achieve X by December, can we revisit in January?"
- ✅ Request it in writing or via email for accountability
- ✅ Ask about grade progression or promotion path

**If there's no real path:**
- ✅ That answer is data — take it seriously
- ✅ Start building external market value now
- ✅ Update your CV and LinkedIn
- ✅ Consider professional certifications (ACA, ACIB, CIBN Fellowship)
- ✅ A competing offer is sometimes the clearest message

**Alternative asks if raise is not possible:**
- Performance bonus
- Transport/housing allowance increase
- Professional development funding
- Grade upgrade without immediate salary change
- Expanded role with title change (sets up next raise)

## Slide 8: The Wealth Math

### Same ₦100,000. Very different outcomes depending on where it comes from.

**₦100K Monthly Raise (₦1.2M annually)**

✅ Compounds into every future raise (10% next year = ₦10k more monthly)  
✅ Boosts bonus (if 20% of base, that's ₦240k more annually)  
✅ Increases RSA Pension match (8% employer + 10% employee on higher base)  

**→ After 20 years at 7% growth:**
- Direct salary increase alone: **~₦52.4M** extra earned
- With bonuses compounding: **~₦62M+** total impact
- Additional pension contribution: **~₦3.8M** in your RSA

**Total wealth impact: ₦65M+ over your career**

---

**₦100K in Annual Expense Cuts**

✅ One-time saving — doesn't stack  
✅ No effect on bonuses  
✅ No effect on pension match  

**→ After 20 years invested at 7%:**
- ~₦4.4M if invested perfectly
- But you feel the sacrifice every year
- **Zero career leverage**
